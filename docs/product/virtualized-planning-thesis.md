# Virtualized Planning Thesis

## Core Thesis

Hyde virtualizes the brand-planning room. Evidence, expert lenses, assumptions, scenarios, decisions, and learning loops become reusable assets that teams can stress-test, replay, and improve continuously.

The product is not a research chatbot. It is a virtualized marketing planning system: a planning simulator with an evidence graph, expert lenses, decision memory, and confidence checks built around the way brand teams already plan.

The durable shift is from one-off workshops and static decks to a living planning environment. A team can ask what changed, why a recommendation was made, which assumptions carry the plan, what would flip the answer, and what execution learning should update the next cycle.

## What Can Be Virtualized

Hyde can virtualize the core ingredients of planning:

- **Time:** Replay prior planning moments, compare current signals to past assumptions, and keep quarterly learning connected to annual strategy.
- **Space:** Let distributed teams inspect the same evidence, scenarios, and trade-offs without needing every expert in the same room.
- **People and lenses:** Preserve the reasoning of commercial, consumer, finance, media, market, and category perspectives as reusable lenses.
- **Decisions:** Turn choices, assumptions, risks, and confidence levels into assets that can be revisited instead of buried in slides.
- **Evidence:** Maintain a graph of claims, sources, lineage, contradictions, and freshness so trust is earned rather than asserted.
- **Workshops and processes:** Convert planning rituals into repeatable, inspectable workflows instead of isolated meeting outcomes.
- **Execution learning:** Feed in-market results, activations, and misses back into the next recommendation cycle.

Virtualizing **one person** means capturing a useful expert lens that others can query, challenge, and apply consistently. Virtualizing **one decision** means preserving the evidence, assumptions, alternatives, and confidence bar behind a choice, so the team can replay or stress-test it later.

Virtualizing **one process** means turning a planning ritual, such as setting a growth driver, into a guided workflow with repeatable inputs, review points, and outputs. Virtualizing **multiple processes** means connecting MBP, quarterly activation, A&P allocation, channel choices, and learning loops into one planning system rather than separate decks and meetings.

## Diageo / MBP Wedge

The wedge is Marketing Business Planning (MBP), anchored in the real language of Diageo planning: Must-Dos, Growth Drivers, A&P allocation, occasions, channels, markets, and quarterly activations.

Start narrow: one brand, one market, and one Must-Do or Growth Driver. The first product moment should help a team set, inspect, and stress-test that growth driver against available evidence, expert lenses, and scenario assumptions. From there, Hyde can expand across adjacent MBP choices without overcomplicating the first experience.

Jim's feedback sharpens the wedge:

- **Data is thin:** The system should make evidence limits visible and useful, not pretend the data is complete.
- **Confidence and trust are key:** Users need to see why Hyde believes something, where it is uncertain, and what would change the answer.
- **Do not overcomplicate:** The entry point should feel like MBP work the team already recognizes, not a new analytics operating model.
- **Help teams set and stress-test Growth Drivers:** Hyde should support the growth-driver conversation directly, including risks, assumptions, and alternatives.
- **Support in-year querying:** Once a plan is live, teams should ask what has changed, whether a signal affects the plan, and which assumption needs review.

## Verification And Confidence Model

Executives need a simple answer to "why should I trust this?" Hyde should answer in three parts:

1. **Agreed bar and intervals:** Before the system recommends, the team agrees what "good enough to act" means. Outputs should show confidence ranges or clear confidence bands, not false precision.
2. **Provenance and lineage:** Every recommendation should point back to its sources, assumptions, expert lenses, and transformations. Users should be able to see the chain from claim to evidence.
3. **Robustness and sensitivity:** Hyde should show whether the answer holds when assumptions change, which variables matter most, and which counterfactuals would flip the recommendation.

In plain executive language: Hyde does not claim perfect certainty. It shows the evidence base, the confidence bar, the fragile assumptions, and the tests that make a planning choice more or less trustworthy.

## Demo Implications

The Wednesday demo should tell the story as a progression:

1. Start from a familiar MBP slide or planning question.
2. Reveal the virtualized layers behind it: evidence, expert lenses, assumptions, scenarios, and decisions.
3. Surface the top risks and candidate Growth Drivers.
4. Show confidence, evidence, and provenance for a recommendation.
5. Run a counterfactual or simulation that changes a key assumption.
6. Ask an in-year query, such as whether a new market signal should change the plan.

The caveats should be explicit. Hyde is not predicting the future. It is helping teams make directional confidence checks, test whether a plan is robust enough, and keep human experts in control of the decision.

## Product Surfaces

- **Growth Driver Workbench:** The main MBP surface for setting, comparing, and stress-testing Growth Drivers for a brand, market, and Must-Do.
- **Evidence / Asset Explorer:** A graph-backed view of evidence, claims, sources, assumptions, decisions, and reusable expert lenses.
- **Confidence & Sensitivity:** An executive-friendly view of confidence bands, evidence strength, assumption sensitivity, and what would flip the answer.
- **Simulation / Counterfactuals:** A planning simulator for scenario questions, trade-offs, A&P allocation pressure, channel shifts, occasion changes, and market movement.
- **Plan Chat:** A governed query layer for in-year questions over the plan, evidence graph, decisions, and latest signals.

## Working Principle

Hyde should make the planning room reusable. The product wins when teams can move from "we had a good workshop" to "we have a living planning asset we can inspect, challenge, simulate, and improve."
